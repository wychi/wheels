## Tile sizing recipe (228 KB SMEM cap)

**Einsum tile (WGMMA-native BLOCK_M × BLOCK_N × BLOCK_K = 128 × 256 × 64):**
- `A` tile: `128 × 64 × bf16 = 16 KB`
- `B` tile: `256 × 64 × bf16 = 32 KB`
- Per-stage AB cost: `48 KB`; with `num_stages=4`: `192 KB` ← fits in 228 KB.
- Acc (fp32 in registers): `128 × 256 × 4 = 128 KB` of register pressure — split across 2 warpgroups.
- **2 warp-groups** in producer/consumer specialization (`num_warps=8` per CTA).
- TMA descriptors handle the 2D loads; `tlx.async_copy` with the WGMMA shim is required.

**B+C-fused projection tile:**
- 16×16 fused intermediate buffer (`H × bf16 × 16 × 16`) ≈ 64 KB; fits with one stage of double-buffering. **Viable on H100.**

## Specialization opportunities
- **WGMMA + warp-specialization** (TMA producer + WGMMA consumer): canonical Hopper pattern; expect 70–80% of peak.
- **TMA multicast** for `K`-tile broadcast across CTAs in the same cluster (cluster size 2–4) — saves ~20% L2 pressure on the einsum.
- **Stream-K / persistent CTA** schedule for einsum at non-power-of-2 shapes (`S=768`) — 5–10% speedup if einsum is on the critical path.
- **Skip fp8** even though it's available: TriMul activations are roughly Cauchy (heavy-tailed) and saturate the e4m3 ±448 range, which damages the gating units. Keep bf16.

## Portability concerns
- This is the canonical target for the current uTLX wheel — patch coverage is best here. WGMMA codegen, TMA shims, named-barrier patches all exercised.
- Cluster-launch / DSMEM may need `cluster_dims` patches; check `runner/tlx_patches.py` for the resolved patch set.

## Top-3 recommendations
1. Tile einsum **128×256×64**, 2 WGs producer/consumer, `num_stages=4`, TMA loads.
2. **Apply B+C fusion** on the projection epilogue — saves ~0.13 ms of intermediate I/O (~3–5% e2e win).
3. **Re-profile after the S1 thin-K config change** (see Profiling Validation) — the current S1 leaves ~3× on the table at the smallest shape.

The kernel profiled is *just* the 5-projection GEMM step in the implementation's S1 pipeline; it's not the entire S1. ### Root cause of the underutilization (this shape)

The profiled tile config (`BM=256, BN=128, BK=64, NUM_STAGES=3, NUM_MMA_GROUPS=2`) is tuned for the largest shape's K=384 (6 K-iterations per output tile, deep WS pipeline reaches steady state). At the smallest shape's K=128, the same config gives:

- Only **2 K-iterations per output tile** → producer/consumer pipeline never reaches steady state, fills + drains on every tile.
- **Stall Barrier = 24%** of warp cycles (typical well-balanced WS-GEMM is 5–10%).
- **Stall Long Scoreboard = 54%** — consumers blocked waiting for SMEM writes.
- **GMMA stall ≈ 0%** — WGMMA is starved, not back-pressured.
- 154 reg/thread + 213 KB SMEM both pin the kernel to **1 CTA/SM**, intentional for warp-spec but limits occupancy at the small shape.

This is a property of *this kernel at this shape*, not of H100 in general — the largest shape (K=384) almost certainly avoids the WS-pipeline starvation pathology. See "End-to-End Measurements" for verification.

### Recommended tile-config changes for `matmul_kernel_tlx_ws` (this kernel only)

For projection-heavy shapes (D=128, K=D in the GEMM):
1. **`NUM_STAGES=3 → 1 or 2`** — deep pipeline doesn't help when there are 2 K-iterations.
2. **`BK=64 → BK=128`** — collapses inner K-loop to 1 trip, eliminates producer/consumer ping-pong.
3. **`BM=256 → BM=128`** — better load balance (currently 5 N-tiles don't divide 132 SMs cleanly).
4. **`NUM_MMA_GROUPS=2 → 1`** — nothing to interleave with 2 K-iters.
5. **`EPILOGUE_SUBTILE=False → True`** — overlap C TMA store with next tile's load.

For D=384 shapes (K=384), keep the current recipe.

**Action item:** wire two compiled tile configs into the persistent dispatcher — a "thin-K" path for D=128 and a "fat-K" path for D=384.

### Implications for the doc's design (extrapolated, unvalidated)

Treat the items below as hints from a single-shape, single-kernel data point — not as validation of the planning doc.

- **45% HBM efficiency at this shape** suggests the doc's planning assumption of 60–75% may be optimistic. But this kernel hits 45% partly because of a known tile-config issue at K=128, not a fundamental ceiling. At larger K, achieved BW likely climbs.
- **The doc's "S1 IO ≈ 1.2 GB fused" estimate** assumes a fusion strategy that doesn't match the actual implementation. Re-derive IO when designing the actual fused kernel; don't trust the 1.2 GB number without re-deriving.
- **The doc's compute-vs-memory bottleneck classification at the largest shape** was based on a low IO estimate. At the corrected ~2.5 GB IO, even the largest shape may stay memory-bound — but neither has been measured.
- **B+C fusion's "3–5% e2e win"** has not been measured. The actual implementation explicitly rejected B+C-style fusion in favor of fp32 cuBLAS for the einsum and final linear, citing precision concerns on cauchy-distributed inputs at S ≥ 512. Any B+C-fused bf16 design needs an accuracy-budget test before claiming the speedup.

### The big finding: precision tax dominates

**~62% of end-to-end time at the largest shape is precision-related work that wouldn't exist in our doc's all-bf16 fused design:**
- 25% upcasting `L, R` from bf16 to fp32 for the bmm (1.07 GB write each — pure HBM bandwidth)
- 16% downcasting the fp32 bmm output back to bf16
- 21% running the einsum + final linear in fp32 cuBLAS (bf16 WGMMA would be ~3× faster at the same FLOP count)

The author chose this trade deliberately — the file comment at line 1565 says: *"bf16 bmm over K=seq_len accumulates ~sqrt(K)·0.4% relative error, which exceeds atol=2e-2 for sl ≥ 512 with cauchy-tailed values. TF32's 10-bit mantissa cuts that by ~10×."*

The accuracy data above confirms the concern is real: shapes 1 and 3 (sl ≥ 512, cauchy in shape 1) sit at 0.020–0.022 max abs err — within the 2e-2 budget but with very little margin. A pure-bf16 design **may not pass** without per-shape mitigations (per-batch input scaling, clamping before the bf16 cast, fp32 accumulator on the einsum tile).

### Updated implications for the planning doc

| Doc claim | Status after e2e measurement |
|---|---|
| "Realistic e2e on H100 ≈ 1.5–1.8 ms" | **Wrong by ~6×** for the current implementation. Achievable for a redesigned all-bf16 kernel only if accuracy holds. |
| "S1 dominates wall time at ≈ 0.7–0.85 ms" | **Wrong** — actual S1 in this implementation (LN-stats + 5-proj GEMM + fused_gate_ln) totals ~2.9 ms (29% of e2e). Dominant cost is precision casts, not S1 compute or S1 IO. |
| "B+C fusion saves ~3–5% e2e" | **Likely an underestimate** — the bf16↔fp32 round-trip alone is 41% of e2e. A fusion that keeps everything in bf16 and folds the post-einsum work into the einsum kernel could plausibly save 30–50%, conditional on accuracy. |
| "Einsum is at the roofline knee at S=1024" | **Wrong for this implementation** — the einsum runs as fp32 cuBLAS, not bf16 WGMMA. Achieves ~250 GFLOP / 1.11 ms = 247 TF/s (vs 989 bf16 / ~378 TF32 peak). A bf16 WGMMA einsum could plausibly hit ~0.3–0.4 ms. |

### Honest verdict

This implementation is a **safe, correctness-first baseline.** It accepts a 60%+ precision tax to guarantee atol=2e-2 on cauchy shapes. The planning doc's design is the **performance-first alternative** — potentially 2–3× faster, but its viability depends on whether bf16 numerics survive the accuracy gate at S ≥ 512.

**Next concrete step:** prototype a single fused S1 + bf16 WGMMA einsum + B+C-fused S3 (skip the fp32 path), measure accuracy on the cauchy shapes, then compare wall-time. Until that prototype exists, the doc's design is an architectural prediction, not a validated win.

## Toolchain caveat

Every IR-based MPP analysis (`barrier`, `tma`, `register_mapping`, `sm_occupancy_heatmap`, `latency_breakdown`) failed with "Parse MLIR file failed" — MPP's bundled Triton (`3.6.0+fb.beta`, vendored at `/usr/local/devfeature/fbpkg/triton-mpp-h100#175/mpp.cli-inplace#link-tree/triton`) cannot parse the `ttg.warp_specialize` op, `#ttg.nvmma_shared` SMEM layouts, or `!tt.tensordesc<...>` types that uTLX emits via Triton 3.7.0. NCU + capture-time JSON were the only usable signals. Installing a newer Triton in the venv does not help — MPP ignores the active venv and uses its vendored copy. To get full MPP coverage on uTLX kernels we need an fbpkg bump on `triton-mpp-h100`.
